A lot of people believe the GDPR bans cold email. It doesn't. It asks you to have a good reason to process someone's details, to be open about it, and to stop when they ask. Most B2B outreach can meet that bar. *This is general information, not legal advice.*

## Pick a lawful basis

For business-to-business prospecting the usual basis is **legitimate interest**. That means three things:

1. You have a genuine interest (for example, offering a product that's relevant to their job).
2. Emailing them is necessary to pursue it.
3. Their interests don't override yours, which mostly comes down to being relevant and easy to opt out from.

Write this down. LeadsGen lets you record a short legitimate-interest note on every lead, and workspaces can require one before anything is sent.

## Check the electronic marketing rules too

In the UK and EU, the ePrivacy rules (PECR in the UK) sit alongside the GDPR. They're stricter for individual consumers than for corporate subscribers. As a rule of thumb, emailing a named person at a company about their work is treated very differently from emailing someone's personal address.

## Be open and easy to leave

Every email should say who you are, why you're getting in touch, and how to stop hearing from you. Honour opt-outs immediately and permanently. LeadsGen adds an unsubscribe link and your business address to every email, and checks the suppression list right before each send.

## Keep only what you need

Don't hoard data. Delete leads you never contacted after a reasonable period, and answer access and erasure requests promptly. Retention rules and one-click data requests in LeadsGen make this routine rather than a scramble.
